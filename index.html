Function first, then form. A beautiful room that doesn't work — poor circulation, inadequate lighting, uncomfortable furniture — will never feel right. Start with how the space will be used, then layer aesthetics on top.

Use three layers: ambient (general light from ceiling or large pendants), task (reading lamps, under-cabinet lights), and accent (picture lights, floor uplights). Dimmers on every circuit give you control throughout the day.

Light-toned woods, polished stone, glass, and metallics reflect light and create a sense of openness. Avoid heavy, dark materials on floors and large surfaces. Consistency of material across rooms also makes a small home feel larger.

About Elements Shape Living

Elements Shape Living is an independent editorial publication founded by architects, designers, and writers who believe that good design is a form of generosity. We publish in-depth articles on residential architecture, interior design, materials, and the craft of making spaces that endure.

Our contributors include practicing architects, interior designers, historians, and material scientists. We are reader-supported and never accept sponsored content.
